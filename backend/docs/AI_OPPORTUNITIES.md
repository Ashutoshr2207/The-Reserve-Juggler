## 2. AI evidence and what it does not establish

The supplied research favors deterministic numerical logic and optional language assistance. Retain that as a baseline preference, not a categorical claim that LLMs cannot contribute to cash management. The original BIS payment-agent study reports useful prompted behavior in stylized scenarios and notes occasional variation as complexity grows. It does not establish actual-bank performance or safe autonomous money movement. A pretrained assistant therefore merits a controlled comparison only if it solves a defined operator task. [BIS original study, November 2025](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1310-ai-agents-cash-management-payment-systems.pdf)

**[FACT]** Amazon's Chronos-2 model card lists an Apache-2.0 license, 120M parameters, quantile forecasting, covariate support, and CPU/GPU inference. These are publisher descriptions, not local throughput or accuracy findings. **[INFERENCE]** a pretrained time-series model is a relevant comparator to fitting a local model; a generic chat model is not the only pretrained option. Pin revision and check data overlap before interpreting any public benchmark. [Chronos-2 model card](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-2)

## 4. Training and pretrained comparison branches

All training candidates are **PROPOSED**, because no accepted scope requires a trained model. No branch is REQUIRED. If the human later makes measured predictive allocation a mandatory feature, a forecast evaluation becomes required; model fitting still competes with pretrained/statistical alternatives.

### T1 — fit a small probabilistic flow model: PROPOSED / EXPERIMENT

- **Objective [HYPOTHESIS]:** estimate next-horizon gross outflow quantiles (for example 1h, 4h and end-of-operating-day, chosen with owner) using only information available at decision time. Evaluate both gross flows and resulting policy-constrained accessible liquidity. Do not conflate a net-flow prediction with immediately accessible cash.
- **Data:** D01 preferred; D02 only after rights verification for a daily proxy. Proposed minimum discovery request is 6–12 months of timestamped history across normal and peak cycles, not a guarantee of tail coverage or a universal sufficiency threshold. Assess timezone/DST, resets, returns, missing periods, account changes and holidays before model choice.
- **Baseline:** fixed-policy B0, seasonal same-hour/day estimate, rolling empirical quantiles; then simple quantile regression/boosted trees. Compare an unmodified pretrained model on the same splits. RAG retrieves policy facts; it is not a substitute for forecasting training or numeric ground truth.
- **Metrics [TARGET]:** pinball loss at preselected quantiles, interval coverage/width, MAE for scale, shortfall count/severity/time, average buffer, missed obligations, and simulated net economics. Avoid MAPE when targets may be zero or negative. Calibrate per horizon/segment; uncertainty and scarce tail evidence must be reported.
- **Split:** proposed earliest 60% train, next 20% calibration/selection, final 20% locked test; use rolling-origin evaluation within development and purge overlapping forecast windows at boundaries. Fit imputers/scalers on training only. Preserve as-known rather than revised features. Stress periods stay in evaluation; do not remove difficult months just to improve scores. Compare paired days and report uncertainty (e.g., day-block resampling), not just a single mean.
- **Annotation:** no hand labeling needed for observed future sums once timing is trustworthy; a treasury reviewer must adjudicate availability/obligation semantics and exceptional corrections. Proposed review 50–100 sampled intervals plus all discrepancy types; actual count unknown until data profiling.
- **Compute/budget [ASSUMPTION]:** local 4–8 CPU cores / 16–32GB RAM, 4–40 aggregate CPU-hours for a capped first comparison, $0–$40 incremental compute at an assumed $0–$1/hour. Data cleanup/review 16–40h at assumed $25–$75/hour = $400–$3,000, excluding data licensing. These are envelopes, not validated hardware requirements or approved spend.
- **Feasibility experiment [TARGET]:** after A01/A06 gates, cap at three model families, fixed seeds and locked splits. Candidate target ≥10% reduction in held-out pinball loss over strongest simple baseline and no worse count/severity of shortfalls at matched average buffer; show net value after costs. Policy owner must approve tolerances. No result or fitted model exists. Fail/defer if baseline is adequate, rights fail, calibration is weak, or data is unrepresentative.

### T2 — pretrained time-series inference and optional adaptation: PROPOSED

Run zero-shot inference before any fine-tuning. Use T1's exact data, split and metrics; compare against local classical models. Check artifact license, pinned revision, dependency security, calibration, missing-history behavior and pretraining overlap. Public benchmark results do not count as this project's accuracy. Pretrained inference may be worth testing even when D01 is too small for deep-model training; it still needs relevant held-out evidence.

**[ASSUMPTION]** first inference trial: existing CPU or 1–10 accelerator-hours at assumed $1–$4/hour ($1–$40 if rented), plus 4–12h integration/review. If and only if persistent residual errors justify adaptation, cap an initial fine-tuning comparison at 10–40 accelerator-hours ($10–$160 at the same hypothetical rates), with isolated checkpoints and early stopping. Local memory/hardware, download access, task runtime and actual rates remain untested. Do not allocate GPU resources or claim low latency from publisher throughput.

**Recommendation:** experiment zero-shot after rights/evaluation gates; defer fine-tuning. RAG does not adapt time-series dynamics. Training a deep model from scratch would need far more representative series and rigorous comparison; no evidence currently justifies it.

### T3 — extraction/generative fine-tuning: PROPOSED / DEFER

Objective could be correct extraction of policy limits/exceptions or evidence-grounded summaries. Compare a form/template, pretrained extraction with schema validation, and retrieval of approved clauses first. RAG handles changing policy facts without storing them in model weights; fine-tuning might improve format/task behavior but does not guarantee factuality or current policy.

No task-labeled corpus is supplied. A possible feasibility set is 100–300 independently labeled clauses across multiple document families, with at least 50 held-out clauses from distinct documents; this is an experiment design, not a sufficiency claim for training. Two domain reviewers adjudicate disagreement on amounts, entity scope, exceptions and effective dates. Use exact field match and clause citation correctness, not prose similarity as a proxy for correctness. Version/family separation prevents near-duplicate leakage.

**[ASSUMPTION]** labeling 20–60h and experiment/review 8–24h at $25–$75/hour = $700–$6,300 labor; optional compute ceiling 10–40 accelerator-hours at $1–$4/hour = $10–$160. These ranges exclude commercial model/data license fees and are not an approval. Without enough rights-cleared examples or measurable residual error, do not fit a model. Prefer human-approved forms or pretrained/RAG assistance.

### T4 — foundation training / RL policy learning: PROPOSED / DEFER

No justified objective, corpus, budget, or measured deficiency in simpler methods supports foundation-model training. A speculative cost number would be misleading: no defensible full-training estimate can be produced without parameter scale, tokens/series volume, hardware and experiment plan. Full training is outside proposed Phase 02 scope.

RL requires a credible environment and reward aligned with real constraints. A simulator that rewards reduced cash while overlooking failed obligations can produce an unsafe policy. If future research warrants it, first reproduce deterministic stress behavior with a reviewed reward and capacity-constrained funding; cap a feasibility pilot to 20–100 CPU-hours at assumed $0–$1/hour and 20–40h domain/agent work before any larger run. This small simulator budget is **not** a foundation-training budget. No training or simulator construction is authorized/performed now.

## 5. Quality evaluation and release boundaries

| Component | Proposed evaluation / acceptance target | Failure handling |
|---|---|---|
| Authoritative evidence/policy core | All reviewed finite cases exactly reproduce decision, currency, policy version and source references; no unsafe allow on seeded missing/stale/unknown cases | Block or unknown; no model fallback to manufacture coverage |
| Extraction | Held-out documents scored by field/exception, exact source span and effective-date interpretation; zero unreviewed critical fields reach active policy | Require human approval and preserve correction history; OCR uncertainty visible |
| Search/RAG | Start with 50 answerable/unanswerable/adversarial questions over fictional or permitted policies; compare keyword vs hybrid. Target ≥95% recall@5 on answerable cases, 100% supported critical statements and no cross-tenant retrieval in test suite | Abstain on unsupported/ambiguous questions; expand retrieval/permissions tests after observed failures |
| Generated explanations | Numeric/currency/date/decision consistency checked against canonical record; target zero material contradiction across 50 reviewed cases and ≥20% less review time than templates if generation is retained | Suppress unsupported sentence and show template; all figures remain available without prose |
| Forecasts | T1 loss/calibration and downstream risk/economics metrics; no “accuracy %” invented | Revert to conservative baseline on stale inputs, drift or failed evaluation |
| Assistant/tool workflow | Deny forbidden roles/accounts; inject malicious policy text; retry/timeouts/replayed state; no live execution endpoint exposed in initial scope | Tool gateway enforces scope; record denial; stop and request operator review when state is ambiguous |

All thresholds above are proposals; tests have not run. Zero observed failures in a small suite is not a reliability guarantee. Human review time includes checking source claims, not merely reading a polished explanation. Quality of generated prose cannot establish truth of balances.

## 6. Latency, cost and integration scenarios

**[TARGET]** deterministic per-decision computation budget <100ms on a later specified local test machine, excluding ingestion/signing I/O; full evidence-update freshness is measured from bank as-of time and ingestion time separately. No runtime measured. Ingestion delays, bank availability and retries may dominate.

For optional assistance, propose <10s p95 for an ordinary explanation, timeout at 15s and show a template; batch document extraction may take up to 60s per document before user review. For T1/T2, propose hourly/batched forecasting with p95 <5s per small account batch after model warmup, then profile against account count/context length. These are UX design targets, not model SLA claims. Keep assistance off the policy decision's critical path.

**[ASSUMPTION]** use hypothetical rates $1–$5 per million input tokens and $4–$20 per million output tokens, not any named provider's current price. For 2,000 input + 500 output tokens, one call costs $0.004–$0.020. At 1,000 calls/month, $4–$20; at 20,000 calls, $80–$400. Add retries, retrieval, embeddings, logging, taxes and provider minimums separately. If reasoning tokens/cached input are priced differently, recalculate from the chosen official tariff before approval. No provider selected or queried.

A one-time corpus example: 1M tokens × a hypothetical embedding rate of $0.02–$0.20/M = $0.02–$0.20 for embedding computation only; secure storage, metadata filters, document processing and human review are not included. Vector-database hosting is unnecessary until keyword/structured retrieval fails a defined evaluation. Do not confuse low token cost with low total operating cost; connector/support/reviewer effort is in RESEARCH.md scenarios.

Integration needs are explicit typed inputs, deterministic validation, versioned model/prompt/policy artifacts, token/latency/error metering, access-scoped retrieval, offline reproducibility where possible, and a tested template/rule fallback. Hosted-model retention, location, provider subprocessors, isolation and logging terms are unknown until procurement. Self-hosting avoids some external transfer but adds patching, memory, model security and monitoring burdens; it is not inherently private or compliant.

## 7. Privacy, access control and failure analysis

**[FACT]** OWASP identifies direct/indirect prompt injection and notes that RAG and fine-tuning do not fully mitigate it. **[INFERENCE]** bank documents, transaction descriptions, retrieved pages and OCR content must be handled as untrusted data. A language model may propose a query; application code must enforce permissions and validate its arguments. [OWASP primary guidance](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)

Proposed controls: minimize customer-level fields; use aggregate flows when adequate; scope authorization by tenant/entity/account/role before retrieval and again on export; enforce effective policy version; record source provenance; prevent raw balances or identifying data from leaking through traces/caches; isolate signing keys from model tools; use read-only connectors and bounded retry/timeouts; separate maker/checker and policy approval; review retention/deletion implications before append-only storage. An auditor's evidence export must not include another entity's private transactions. None of these controls is implemented or audited in Phase 01.

Important failures: fabricated cause for an outflow; plausible extraction of the wrong exception; stale policy retrieved; currency/units confused; image/OCR instruction injection; training on corrected history unavailable at decision time; seasonal model failure in a run; correlated data between train/test; shared cache leaking another tenant; model/provider outage; duplicate execution after retry; compromised signing key; model confidence mistaken for verified liquidity. Conventional failure states remain useful even if the AI is removed.

## 8. Next decision

**Adopt:** deterministic policy/evidence computation and templates as the evaluation baseline; structured search when a corpus exists. **Experiment:** extraction only if document burden is demonstrated; quantile forecasting/pretrained inference only after rights and evaluation gates; RAG only if structured/keyword lookup is inadequate. **Defer:** autonomous financial agents, complex orchestration, fine-tuning, RL, multi-party ML and training from scratch. **No AI:** authoritative money arithmetic, legal approval, balance authenticity, key management and final access control.

Phase 02 should validate the workflow and settle whether any AI branch has enough incremental value to enter a later prototype plan. Carry A01–A07 forward. If the core evidence workflow passes but AI adds no value, proceed without AI. Desk research establishes candidate tools and data limitations; it cannot establish task accuracy, lawful access, buyer demand, acceptable risk, or safe real-world autonomy.
